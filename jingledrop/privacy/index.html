Privacy Policy

Jingle Drop · last updated 28 October 2026

The short version: I collect nothing, from anyone of any age. Google shows the ads, and every ad request is set for children.

Jingle Drop is free and shows ads from Google AdMob: an optional short video, and now and then a short ad between runs. Every ad request is marked as directed at children, for every player, and the game does not use the advertising ID, on Android or on iPhone. To show an ad, Google receives some technical data from the phone, listed below. One optional purchase, Remove ads, is handled entirely by Google Play on Android and by the App Store on iPhone. There are no analytics and no accounts, and your high scores stay on your phone.

This policy explains that in full. Jingle Drop is a Christmas game for Android phones and iPhone made by Nilbyte Studio ("I", "me"), for everyone from 3 to 99. Its Android package name and its iPhone bundle ID are both com.nilbytestudio.jingledrop. Where the two versions differ, this policy says so.

What the game stores on your phone

Jingle Drop keeps a few small settings in its own private storage on your phone:

  • Your ten best runs: the score, how many gifts you delivered, your best streak, and the date and time you played
  • Whether you have seen the how-to-play cards, whether sound is on, and the language you picked
  • Whether you have finished a run before (the very first run never offers the video)
  • Whether you have bought Remove ads

Jingle Drop sends none of this anywhere. I never see it, and uninstalling the app deletes it. A bought Remove ads comes back from Google Play or the App Store when you reinstall.

Advertising

Jingle Drop is free and funded by ads from Google AdMob, in two places only:

  • "Keep flying?" When Santa loses his last lantern, the game may offer a short video. Watching it to the end gives one more lantern; "No thanks" simply ends the run. It is offered at most once per run, and never on your very first run.
  • Between runs. Now and then a short ad appears when you leave the game-over screen: never during play, never when the game starts, never in the first three runs after opening the game, and at most once every four minutes.

Every ad can be closed after a few seconds.

Set for children, for everyone

Jingle Drop has no age question, so it treats every player as a child. Every ad request it makes is marked as directed at children and under the age of consent, and limited to ads rated G, suitable for all ages. For such requests Google does not show personalised ads, meaning ads chosen from your interests or past activity, and does not send the advertising identifier.

Jingle Drop does not use the advertising ID. On Android, the app removes the permission that would let it, or Google's ad software inside it, read that ID.

On iPhone, Jingle Drop never asks to track you. It never shows Apple's "Allow to track" prompt (App Tracking Transparency), so iOS gives the game and Google's ad software no access to the iPhone's advertising identifier (the IDFA), and the game does not use it.

What Google receives

Google's Mobile Ads SDK — not me — collects information in order to deliver and measure the ad and to prevent fraud. Google publishes what that includes, and for Jingle Drop it is the same on Android and iPhone:

  • Your phone's IP address, from which a general location may be estimated
  • App interactions, such as the game being opened, taps and ad views
  • Advertising data: which ads were shown
  • Diagnostic information about how the game and the ad software perform, including crashes
  • A device identifier that works only within one app or one developer's apps: Android's "app set ID", or on iPhone an ID scoped to the app or to its developer. Never the advertising ID

All of it comes from ad requests set for children and limited to ads rated G, as described above.

It is encrypted in transit. Google's handling is governed by Google's privacy policy and its description of how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services. I see none of this data about individuals; my access is limited to aggregate figures in the AdMob dashboard.

In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland

Jingle Drop shows no consent form. Because it never asks for personalised ads, Google serves what it calls limited ads there: ads without any personalisation and without features that rely on an identifier stored on your phone. Google may still use storage on the phone that only serves to detect invalid traffic and fraud.

An ad, if tapped, can open the advertiser's page in your browser. That is the only way anything in the game leads outside it.

Remove ads

Remove ads is a one-time in-app purchase. It stops the ads between runs and makes "Keep flying?" free, without a video. It is entirely optional: the whole game can be played without it.

  • The button sits behind a grown-up check, a times-table question, so young children don't reach the purchase by accident. The store's own purchase approval still applies on top: on Android Google Play's, such as your account password or Family Link; on iPhone the App Store's, such as Face ID, your Apple Account password or Ask to Buy.
  • The purchase is processed entirely by Google Play on Android and by the App Store on iPhone. The store handles the payment and tells the game only whether you own Remove ads; I never see your name, payment details or address. The game records only a yes/no flag on your phone.
  • On iPhone, Restore purchase (behind the same grown-up check) asks the App Store again whether you own Remove ads.
  • Refunds and receipts are handled by Google Play or Apple, not by me.

One honest note: buying Remove ads stops ads being shown, but Google's ad software stays in the game and still starts with it. I would rather write that down than let the purchase imply more than it delivers.

What Jingle Drop does not do

  • No user accounts, sign-up or login — nothing is linked to your identity by me
  • No servers or cloud storage operated by me
  • No analytics or tracking of my own. Jingle Drop does not measure how you play. The analytics, crash-reporting and performance-reporting services of the Unity engine, which the game is built with, are switched off, and Unity's purchasing service is not used
  • No remote crash reporting to me (the ad software's diagnostics, above, go to Google)
  • No use of the advertising ID (Android's, or the iPhone's IDFA), no "Allow to track" prompt, and no personalised ads
  • No chat, no sharing and no way to contact anyone through the game
  • No sale of personal information — I hold none to sell
  • No location, contacts, storage, camera or microphone access

Children

Jingle Drop is made for children as well as adults. It is listed on Google Play for every age group, under Google Play's Families policy, and rated 4+ on the App Store. That is why every ad request is set for children, the ads are rated G, the advertising ID is not used, the iPhone version never asks to track you, and the purchase sits behind a grown-up check.

I collect no personal information from anyone, so I collect none from children. The data Google's ad software receives is listed under Advertising, and Google handles it under its rules for child-directed requests. This is how the game meets Google Play's Families policy, the App Store's rules, and children's privacy laws such as the US COPPA and the GDPR's rules for children.

The game has no chat and no way to contact anyone. Only an ad, if tapped, can open a page outside the game.

Backups

One thing is outside the game's control: your phone's own backup. Jingle Drop itself never sends your game data anywhere, and I never receive it.

  • Android. If you have turned on Android's own device backup, Android may include Jingle Drop's high scores and settings in your backup to your Google account, as it does for most apps, and may restore them if you reinstall. Google runs that backup, under its own terms.
  • iPhone. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or to a computer, the backup includes Jingle Drop's high scores and settings, as it does for most apps, and they come back when you set up an iPhone from that backup. Apple runs iCloud Backup, under its own terms. Deleting the app and installing it again on the same iPhone does not bring them back.

Without such a backup, uninstalling Jingle Drop removes your high scores for good.

Permissions

On Android, Jingle Drop uses internet access and the network state for the ads and for Google Play, and Google Play's billing permission for Remove ads. Google's ad and Play software also adds two technical permissions it needs to work: keeping the phone awake for a moment, and running a short background task. Jingle Drop does not ask for location, contacts, storage, the camera or the microphone.

On iPhone, Jingle Drop asks for no permissions at all: no tracking, location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone, and no notifications.

For completeness: the Android app's manifest also declares one internal, signature-level permission that Android's own support libraries add so that only the app itself can send messages to its own components. It is not shown to you, and it gives the game no access to your data or your phone.

Contacting support

If you email me, I have your email address and whatever you wrote, kept only as long as it takes to answer you. That correspondence is the only personal information about you I ever hold. Please don't send a child's personal details; a description of the problem and the phone model is all I need.

Retention and deletion

Your high scores and settings stay on your phone for exactly as long as you keep the game. Uninstall it and they go with it. A copy in Android's device backup is managed by Google through your Google account, and a copy in an iPhone backup by Apple (iCloud) or on your own computer. Because I hold no copy, there is no retention period on my side and no deletion request to make to me. Data held by Google in connection with the ads is subject to Google's own retention policies, and purchase records are held by Google Play or Apple.

Your rights

Data-protection laws including the GDPR and the California Consumer Privacy Act give you rights to access, correct, delete and port your personal information, and to opt out of its sale or sharing.

For your game data, Jingle Drop satisfies these by design: I never receive it, so there is nothing of it on my side to access, correct or export, and deleting it is uninstalling the game. For the data Google's ad software receives, Google answers requests about it; see Google's privacy policy. If you email me about a support question, you can ask me to delete that correspondence at any time.

The advertising layer is different, and I would rather be precise than flattering. Google determines how the data its Mobile Ads SDK collects is used, and acts as a controller in its own right. Because I chose to embed that SDK, European case law treats me as a joint controller with Google for the collection and transmission of that data — not for what Google subsequently does with it. I hold none of this data and cannot access it.

Where the law expects joint controllers to have an arrangement between them, that role is served by the Google terms I accepted to serve ads. In practice: Google is responsible for the data it collects and for answering requests about it. I am responsible for this policy being accurate, for setting every request as child-directed, and for the fact that ads run in the game at all. If you are unsure who to approach, write to me and I will point you to the right place.

International transfers

Google processes advertising data in the United States and other countries. Those transfers rely on Google's own safeguards — its certification under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and, where that does not apply, the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses. Google publishes the current position in its privacy policy. If you would rather no such transfer happened, buying Remove ads stops the ads being shown.

You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority. Nilbyte Studio is established in Sweden, so the lead authority is Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten (IMY). If you live or work elsewhere in the EEA or the UK, you may complain to your own country's supervisory authority instead — you do not need to raise it with me first. Though if something here concerns you, I would rather hear about it and fix it.
