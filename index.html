Work

The apps

Two so far. Both solve a problem I actually had, both keep your data on your own device, and neither one asks you to make an account or pay monthly.

  • Live iOS · Android

    ForeWind

    A golf shot calculator that tells you how far the ball will actually go. Point the phone down your target line, and it combines the live wind from your exact position with the shot direction to give you a real playing distance instead of a guess.

    • Live weather from MET Norway, cached so it works on a patchy course signal
    • Cosine-interpolated wind model — no crude headwind/tailwind buckets
    • Built-in compass with calibration; metres or yards, m/s or mph
    • Free with a banner ad; one-time purchase removes it for good
    • .NET MAUI
    • C#
    • EF Core
    • SQLite
    • xUnit v3
    App Store Google Play Support
  • Coming soon Android · iOS

    RefundHound

    Track the refunds you are owed, and get nagged until the money actually lands. Merchants promise "5–10 business days" and count on you forgetting. Log a refund in fifteen seconds and the app remembers for you.

    • Asks "did it arrive?" on the day it was due, then escalates: check account → contact support → dispute
    • No account, no email access, no bank connection — everything stays on the phone
    • Free for three refunds at a time; one-time unlock for unlimited, stats and CSV export
    • .NET MAUI
    • C#
    • EF Core
    • SQLite
    • xUnit v3
    Support In App Store review · finishing Google Play testing

About

I build what I want to use

I'm Johan, a software developer on the Swedish west coast. Nilbyte Studio is where I ship my own apps. Every one starts the same way: I run into something annoying, look for an app that fixes it, and find only things that want an account, a subscription and an analytics SDK first.

So the subject matter can be anything — wind on a golf course, refunds that never arrive, whatever comes next. The common thread isn't a market or an industry, it's that I wanted the thing to exist and was willing to build it properly.

I don't take client work. No agency services, no contracting, no "let's build your idea". That keeps the roadmap short and means I only answer to the people using the apps.

I do everything myself: design, code, store releases and the unglamorous maintenance afterwards. Both apps keep what you put into them on your own device — I run no server and have no copy of your data.

Being straight about the money: ForeWind is free and shows a banner ad, with a one-time purchase to remove it. RefundHound is free for three refunds, with a one-time unlock for the rest. Those ads come from Google's SDK and it does its own tracking, which is exactly why buying the removal takes it out of the app entirely. Neither app has a subscription, and I add no analytics of my own.
