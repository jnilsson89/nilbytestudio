The problem

The money is owed. Remembering it is the hard part.

A returned parcel, a cancelled booking, a double charge. Each one comes with a vague promise and no receipt you'll ever look at again. Miss the window to dispute it and the merchant simply keeps the money.

  • It tells you what to do next

    This is the part other trackers skip. Once the promised date has passed, RefundHound stops being a list and starts being useful: check your account, then contact support with a message it writes for you, then how a card dispute works. The advice escalates as the refund ages.

  • One tap to close it out

    When the money lands, mark it arrived and you're done. If it hasn't, say so and RefundHound keeps chasing — every three days, until it either turns up or you decide to write it off. Six reminders, then it stops asking.

  • Watch the money come back

    Mark a refund received in one tap and it moves to your history. The stats screen keeps the only number that matters: how much you've actually recovered since you started keeping track.

What it costs

Free to use. One purchase if you need more room.

  • Three at a time, free

    The free version tracks three active refunds at once. For most people that is three more than they were tracking before.

  • Unlimited, paid once

    RefundHound Unlimited removes the cap. A one-time purchase, not a subscription, and there is nothing else to buy.

  • Nothing else is locked

    History, statistics, receipt photos, the support-message templates and CSV export are free for everyone, forever.

  • No ads

    No banners, no analytics SDK, nothing watching what you shop for.

It never leaves your phone

A refund tracker knows what you bought, what it cost and where you shop. The safest place for that is a database on your own device — so that is the only place it exists. No account, no cloud sync, no bank connection, and no server of mine holding a copy.

Which also means I can't recover your data if you lose your phone. That trade is deliberate, and the CSV export is there so you can keep your own backup.
